# Interviewing a candidate for evidence

Goal: a profile rich enough to write a top-tier CV without inventing anything.

## Conduct
- Ask **one topic at a time**, at most 2–3 tightly related questions per message. Make answering easy: offer examples or options.
- Acknowledge briefly, then dig: the first answer is usually a duty; the follow-up finds the achievement.
- Save what you learn to the profile promptly (don't wait until the end); confirm anything ambiguous.
- Prefer the candidate's own words for facts; you may polish wording later.

## Coverage checklist for the profile
Contact details · target roles/industries/locations · work history (title, employer, dates, location, team/scope, key achievements) · education · certifications · skills (with where they were used) · projects · awards/publications · languages · work authorisation/notice period if relevant · preferences.

## Preferences to capture (store under "## Preferences")
CV length (pages) · spelling (UK/US) · tone (formal/plain/bold) · sections to include/omit · anything not to mention · style taste (classic/modern/compact) · target salary/location constraints if they want them reflected.

## Probing for achievements (STAR + metrics)
- "What was the situation when you started? What changed because of you?"
- "How did you measure success? Roughly how much / how many / how fast?" — accept honest estimates marked as approximate ("~30%").
- "Who used it / how many people, customers, teams?" "What budget or revenue was involved?"
- "What was hard about it? What did you do that others wouldn't have?"
- "What would your manager say was your biggest contribution?"
- "Any recognition — promotion, award, being asked to lead/teach?"

## Gap-driven questions
When a job listing is known, compare its requirements to the profile and ask specifically about each gap: "The role asks for stakeholder management with senior leaders — have you presented to directors or executives? Tell me about one time."
