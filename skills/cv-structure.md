# CV structure

## Default order (experienced candidates)
1. **Header** — name, target headline, email, phone, location (city, country), LinkedIn/portfolio/GitHub if relevant. No photo, date of birth, marital status, or full address unless the candidate's market requires it.
2. **Profile / summary** — 2–4 lines tailored to the role (see "Professional summary").
3. **Experience** — reverse chronological. Each role: title, organisation, location (optional), dates (Mon YYYY – Mon YYYY / Present), 3–6 achievement bullets for recent roles.
4. **Skills** — grouped (e.g. Languages, Platforms, Methods). Only skills evidenced in the profile; prioritise those the job asks for.
5. **Education** — degree, institution, dates; grade/thesis/honours only if strong or recent.
6. Optional: Certifications, Projects, Publications, Awards, Volunteering, Languages.

## Variations
- **Graduates / early career:** Education can come before Experience; Projects become a main section.
- **Career changers:** a "Key skills/achievements" section after the summary can bridge transferable evidence before older, less related experience.
- **Academic CVs** follow different conventions (publications, grants, teaching) and may be long.

## Length
- 1 page: students and graduates, < ~5 years experience (US resumes usually 1 page).
- 2 pages: the norm for experienced professionals in the UK/Europe.
- Respect the candidate's stated preference above all.
- Older roles (10+ years) can be condensed to one line each in an "Earlier career" entry.

## Hygiene
- Consistent date format, tense, punctuation and capitalisation everywhere.
- No tables or multi-column text for core content if ATS parsing matters.
- Every section must earn its space for the target job — cut hobbies unless relevant or distinctive.
