# Tailoring to a job listing

1. **Extract requirements.** From the listing, list (a) must-haves, (b) desirables, (c) implicit expectations (seniority, scope, domain, culture words). Note the exact phrasing of key skills and titles.
2. **Map evidence.** For each requirement find the strongest proof in the profile: a role, project, achievement or qualification. Mark it met / partial / missing.
3. **Prioritise.** The top third of page one should answer "can this person do *this* job?": headline and summary mirror the role title and the 2–3 most important requirements.
4. **Reorder and rewrite**, never fabricate:
   - Order bullets within each role by relevance to the listing.
   - Use the listing's terminology where it truthfully describes the candidate's work ("stakeholder management" vs "client liaison").
   - Expand relevant achievements; compress or drop irrelevant ones.
   - Skills section: put requested skills first; drop noise.
5. **Gaps.** For missing requirements: look for transferable evidence and ask the candidate about it. If there is genuinely none, do not pretend — strengthen adjacent evidence instead.
6. **Keywords.** Include important hard-skill keywords naturally at least once in context (bullet or summary) and in the skills list. Avoid keyword stuffing.

Quality bar: a recruiter skimming for 7 seconds should see the target title, the top requirements, and quantified proof.
